# Practice Plan

## Weekly target

- 8–12 new problems.
- 4–6 spaced re-solves.
- 1 timed mixed set.
- 1 verbal walkthrough recorded or done with another person.
- 1 review of repeated mistakes.

## Session format

Use a 75–90 minute block:

1. Re-solve one old miss: 15 minutes.
2. Solve one new problem: 35–45 minutes.
3. Test and simplify: 10 minutes.
4. Write the pattern, miss, and next review date: 5 minutes.

Stop reading solutions passively. If stuck, take one hint, close it, and finish the implementation yourself.

## Phase 1: core patterns

Build reliable recognition and implementation for arrays, strings, maps, two pointers, sliding windows, prefix sums, stacks, queues, binary search, and linked lists.

Exit test: solve two unseen mediums in 70 minutes with clear complexity analysis and working edge cases.

## Phase 2: trees and graphs

Cover tree traversal, BFS/DFS, topological sort, shortest paths, union-find, heaps, intervals, and graph modeling.

Exit test: choose the right traversal and state representation without looking at tags.

## Phase 3: harder patterns

Cover backtracking, tries, greedy proofs, one- and two-dimensional DP, and design-style data structures.

Exit test: explain the recurrence or invariant before writing code.

## Phase 4: interview mode

Mix topics. Practice clarifying the prompt, talking while coding, testing aloud, and handling follow-ups. Revisit misses more often than solved-first-try problems.

If there are two extra months, use them for weak areas and mock interviews. Do not inflate the problem count with near-duplicates.
